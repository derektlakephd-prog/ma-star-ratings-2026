Characteristics and Financial Implications of Measures Removed From Medicare Advantage Star Ratings

A Research Letter on the 11 quality measures that the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS) removed from the Medicare Advantage (MA) Star Ratings in April 2026, asking how many were topped out and how their removal changes contract ratings and quality bonus payments.

Lake DT, Civelek Y, Bond AM, Khullar D. JAMA Netw Open. 2026;9(10):e2640240.

Derek T. Lake, PhD
Associate Staff, Center for Value-Based Care Research, Cleveland Clinic

What the letter found

CMS removed the measures citing, among other reasons, consistently high performance and limited variation across contracts. Two of them have separate Part C and Part D components, so 13 components leave the rating algorithm. The letter tests that rationale against contract-level performance data and estimates the payment consequences.

A measure is classified as topped out when its bottom-decile contract scores at least 90 on a 0 to 100 attainment scale. By that standard, 5 of the 13 removed components were topped out, compared with none of the 30 retained measures.

Recomputing the 2026 Star Ratings without the removed components lowers the overall rating of 35.1% of the 425 contracts analyzed. Thirty-three contracts covering 2.6 million enrollees fall below the 4-star threshold for quality bonus payments (QBPs), and 6 contracts rise above it.

What the code and data are for

The repository reproduces every number in the letter from public CMS files and lets others reuse the contract-level results.

  • Two Jupyter notebooks replicate the 2026 Star Ratings, remove the 13 components, price QBP eligibility, and check each published value.
  • data/derived/contract_level_results.csv holds ratings, enrollment, and QBP estimates for all 769 contracts in the 2026 Star Ratings. This page is built from the same file and lists the 720 MA contracts among them.
  • A data dictionary, glossary, measure reference, and source list document each step.

For custom analyses or alternative removal scenarios, contact the author.

Notes on the estimates

  • The final rule applies the removals to future Star Ratings years. These estimates apply them to the 2026 ratings to isolate their effect, holding measure stars and the categorical adjustment index fixed and recalibrating reward factor thresholds.
  • QBP values are annual 2026 amounts from county benchmark rates and May 2026 enrollment.
  • Simulated baseline ratings match the CMS-published rating for 431 of the 436 non-disaster contracts that both CMS and the simulation rate.
  • The 64 contracts affected by the CMS disaster policy are excluded because their measure-level adjustments cannot be reproduced from public data.
  • The estimates do not incorporate the ratings CMS recalculated in June 2026 after a court ruling on certain 2026 measures; one cohort contract was recalculated above the bonus threshold.
  • These are research estimates, not official CMS ratings or payment determinations. CMS did not review or endorse this work.
